**Bloating and constipation can produce the same full, tight-belly feeling, but they come from different mechanisms and respond to different fixes.** Bloating is usually excess gas or an oversensitive gut wall; constipation is stool that's too slow, too hard, or too hard to pass. Plenty of people have both at once, which is why generic "just eat more fiber" advice sometimes helps and sometimes makes everything worse. The fastest way to tell them apart is to look at your stool pattern over the last seven days.

## Key takeaways

- **Bloating** is gas distension or a gut wall that pushes out even when gas volume is normal. **Constipation** is defined by stool, not sensation.
- If you're going daily with soft, formed stool (Bristol 3–5) and still feel bloated, it's almost certainly gas or visceral hypersensitivity, not constipation.
- Rome IV defines constipation by stool pattern: fewer than three bowel movements a week, hard or lumpy stools, straining, or the sense you didn't fully empty.
- Constipation-driven bloating usually worsens through the day and eases after a bowel movement. Gas-type bloat eases after burping or passing gas.
- If bloating lasts more than a week, comes with weight loss, bleeding, vomiting, or fever, skip the home remedies and see a clinician.

## What bloating actually is

Bloating has three overlapping drivers. The most common is excess gas from bacterial fermentation in the colon: carbohydrates your small intestine didn't absorb get broken down by microbes and release hydrogen, methane, and CO₂ ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21740-bloated-stomach)). The second is **visceral hypersensitivity**, where a normal volume of gas feels painfully distended because the gut's nerves are turned up. The third is **abdominophrenic dyssynergia**, a reflex where the diaphragm pushes down and the abdominal wall relaxes outward, making the belly visibly swell without much extra gas at all.

About 18% of the general population reports bloating at least once a week, and women are roughly twice as likely as men to feel it ([Oh et al., *Gastroenterology* 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37315866/)). Search interest in bloating peaks every July in the United States, a pattern a 2025 infodemiology study chalked up to summer diet, travel, and hormonal cycles ([Cureus, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12155734/)).

None of those mechanisms require slow stool. You can be bloated and pooping normally.

## What counts as constipation

Constipation isn't a feeling. It's a stool pattern. The Rome IV criteria, used by gastroenterologists worldwide, define functional constipation by at least **two of six** stool-based symptoms over the last three months ([Rome Foundation](https://theromefoundation.org/rome-iv/rome-iv-criteria/)):

- Straining on more than 25% of bowel movements
- Lumpy or hard stools (Bristol types 1 or 2) on more than 25% of bowel movements
- Sensation of incomplete evacuation on more than 25%
- Sensation of anorectal blockage on more than 25%
- Needing manual maneuvers to pass stool
- Fewer than three bowel movements per week

Notice what isn't on that list: *bloating*. Bloating is a frequent symptom **of** constipation, but it's not the definition. The definition lives in your stool, not your belly. That's why checking your stool pattern against the [Bristol Stool Chart](/poopcheck-blog/bristol-stool-chart-types-explained/) is the single most useful thing you can do to sort this out.

## Three patterns that tell you which one you have

### Pattern 1: Gas-type bloating (stool is fine)

You're going at your normal frequency. Stool is Bristol 3–5: soft, formed, easy to pass. But your belly swells through the day and you feel tight after meals. **This is gas or visceral hypersensitivity, not constipation.** Treating it with laxatives won't help and may cause diarrhea. The fixes that work are a low-FODMAP trial, peppermint oil, and (in confirmed cases) rifaximin for SIBO. More in our [bloating causes and remedies guide](/poopcheck-blog/bloating-causes-and-remedies/).

### Pattern 2: Constipation-type bloating (stool is the giveaway)

Fewer than three bowel movements a week, or stool that looks like hard pellets (Bristol 1) or lumpy sausages (Bristol 2). You strain. You feel like you didn't finish. The bloat tends to build through the day and partially relieves after a bowel movement. **This is constipation-driven distension.** Retained stool and the gas it produces physically take up space in the colon. Fix the stool pattern and the bloat usually follows. The first-line moves (fluid, fiber, PEG laxatives) are in our [constipation remedies guide](/poopcheck-blog/constipation-causes-and-remedies/).

### Pattern 3: Both at once (common with IBS)

Stool alternates between hard-and-lumpy and loose-and-watery. Bloating is a near-daily background hum. This is often IBS with mixed bowel habits (IBS-M), which affects stool and gas sensitivity together. Our breakdown of [alternating constipation and diarrhea](/poopcheck-blog/alternating-constipation-diarrhea/) covers the differential. Treating this one as "just constipation" with aggressive laxatives usually tips users into diarrhea, then back into constipation when they overcorrect.

## Why "more fiber" isn't a universal answer

Fiber is the standard advice for both bloating and constipation, and it genuinely helps a subset of each. But fermentable fibers like inulin, chicory root, and FOS feed the exact bacterial fermentation that produces gas-type bloat. Someone with visceral hypersensitivity who doubles their fiber intake often gets *more* bloated, not less ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21740-bloated-stomach)).

Psyllium is the exception: it's the only fiber the American College of Gastroenterology and the American Gastroenterological Association recommend for IBS, and it ferments less than inulin or wheat bran. Start it low (3–5 g once a day), split it from meals, and increase every few days. If your bloat is gas-type with normal stool, start there before anything else.

## When to see a doctor

Bloating alone is rarely dangerous. These combinations are not:

- Bloating that lasts more than a week without improvement ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21740-bloated-stomach))
- Blood in your stool, which Cedars-Sinai gastroenterologist Dr. Mark Pimentel calls "the first thing you look for" ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/stories-and-insights/expert-advice/healthy-bowel-movements))
- Black, tarry stool that iron supplements can't explain
- Unintentional weight loss, night sweats, or fever with your bloating
- Severe, sudden abdominal pain with vomiting
- A clear change from your usual bowel pattern lasting more than two to three weeks, especially after age 45

In the US, acid reflux and bloating searches rise around the holidays, and bloating searches peak in summer, spikes a clinician can help you investigate instead of self-treating ([Cureus, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12155734/)).

## FAQ

**Can you be bloated without being constipated?**
Yes. Most bloating is gas-type or visceral hypersensitivity. If your stool is Bristol 3–5 and you're going at your normal frequency, you're bloated, not constipated.

**Can you be constipated without feeling bloated?**
Yes. Some people with slow-transit constipation go days without passing stool and feel heaviness or fullness rather than classic bloating. The stool pattern is what defines the diagnosis.

**Does drinking water fix bloating or constipation faster?**
Water does more for constipation than bloating. Harder stool pulls water back out of the stool in the colon; adequate fluid keeps stool softer. Water doesn't change gas production or visceral sensitivity.

**Why does my bloat go down overnight?**
Overnight, you're not eating, swallowing air, or recruiting the abdominophrenic reflex. Gas also gradually diffuses or passes. If your bloat is dramatically worse in the evening and gone by morning, it's much more likely gas-type than constipation-type.

**Should I try a laxative if I'm bloated?**
Only if you also meet Rome IV criteria for constipation (hard stool, straining, incomplete emptying, or fewer than three bowel movements a week). Taking a laxative for pure gas-type bloat usually causes diarrhea without touching the bloat.

**When should I test for SIBO?**
If bloating is persistent, worsens with fermentable carbs, and improves on a short low-FODMAP trial, a hydrogen-methane breath test is reasonable to discuss with a gastroenterologist.

## The bottom line

Bloating and constipation live in different places: bloating is in your belly's sensation and gas volume, constipation is in your stool. Check your stool first (frequency, Bristol type, straining), and the right next step usually becomes obvious.

**A one-off foamy stool is almost always a diet or gas problem: fermentable carbs, a sudden fiber jump, lactose, or a few mouthfuls of air you swallowed with lunch. The pattern worth investigating is foam that keeps showing up for more than a couple of weeks, especially when it comes with greasy texture, a pale color, a smell you can't ignore, or weight loss. That combination points to fat malabsorption, which has a short, specific differential and a straightforward workup.**

## Key takeaways

- **Occasional froth is normal.** Gas trapped in stool makes it look bubbly; most of it traces back to high-FODMAP meals, carbonated drinks, lactose, or a fiber ramp ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/foamy-poop)).
- **Persistent foamy, greasy, pale, foul-smelling stool is steatorrhea**: fat that your gut didn't absorb. Clinically, more than **7 grams of fat per 24 hours** on a 100 g/day fat diet is abnormal ([MedlinePlus / NIH](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003588.htm)).
- **Three causes dominate the adult differential**: exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, celiac disease, and bile-flow problems. In children, cystic fibrosis is first on the list ([StatPearls on EPI](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/26572)).
- **Chronic pancreatitis is the most common adult cause of EPI**, with roughly **60–90% of chronic pancreatitis patients** developing exocrine failure within 10–12 years of diagnosis ([StatPearls on EPI](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/26572)).
- **Red flags that override everything**: foam for more than two weeks, visible oil or blood, unintentional weight loss, or persistent diarrhea ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/foamy-poop)).

## What "foamy stool" actually looks like

Foamy or frothy stool is stool with visible air bubbles, either threaded through the mass or floating as a surface film in the toilet bowl. People describe it as:

- Bubbly, like a head on beer
- Light and airy, with a cottony texture
- Pale, yellowish, or greenish rather than normal brown
- Harder to flush than usual, with oily streaks on the bowl

The two features to distinguish from true froth are **mucus** and **oil slick**. Mucus is a stringy, jelly-like coating (see our [mucus-in-stool explainer](/poopcheck-blog/mucus-in-stool/)) and tends to appear with inflammatory conditions. An oil slick is a thin, yellowish film on top of the water with no bubbles; that's the single most specific sign of fat loss and the one that most reliably escalates the workup.

## The benign causes and why they resolve

Most foamy episodes are gas-driven, not fat-driven. The usual suspects:

### 1. FODMAPs and other fermentable carbs

Beans, lentils, onions, cabbage, broccoli, apples, pears, and sugar-free gum (sorbitol, xylitol) feed colonic bacteria that produce hydrogen, methane, and CO₂. Trapped gas turns stool frothy. Our [low-FODMAP diet guide](/poopcheck-blog/low-fodmap-diet-guide/) walks through the elimination-and-reintroduction protocol if a food trigger seems likely.

### 2. Lactose or fructose intolerance

If the small intestine doesn't fully absorb these sugars, bacteria in the colon ferment them, and gas, bloating, and foamy loose stool follow. Symptoms track clearly with the food. Healthline notes that persistent foam from a dietary trigger typically resolves within days of removing it ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/foamy-poop)).

### 3. A sudden fiber jump

Insoluble fiber in whole grains and raw vegetables ferments on arrival. Doubling fiber overnight is the single most common cause of a short run of frothy stools. The gas tapers as the microbiome adjusts; our post on [fiber and stool consistency](/poopcheck-blog/fiber-and-stool-consistency/) covers the usual two-to-four-week adaptation.

### 4. Carbonated drinks and swallowed air

Sparkling water, soda, beer, and eating quickly all introduce air that ends up in stool. If you're not sure whether your stool shape is actually changing from one week to the next, [scan each one with PoopCheck](/download/) and let the AI classify Bristol type and consistency against your own baseline. A dated record of frothy days versus normal days is more useful than memory when the pattern needs explaining.

### 5. A short bout of diarrhea

Fast transit skips water reabsorption and keeps gas trapped. Loose, foamy stool for a day or two after a stomach bug or a stressful week is expected and resolves on its own.

These benign patterns share two features: they're **transient**, and they're **not accompanied by oil, pallor, or weight loss**.

## Steatorrhea: when foam is a signal

Steatorrhea is undigested fat in the stool. It makes stool bulky, pale, greasy, floaty, and difficult to flush, often with a smell that family members notice before you do. The MedlinePlus fecal fat test defines the lab threshold at **more than 7 g of fat per 24 hours** on a controlled 100 g/day fat diet ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003588.htm)).

The MedlinePlus list of causes of excess fecal fat is the clean differential to work through:

- Chronic pancreatitis or pancreatic cancer
- Celiac disease
- Crohn's disease
- Cystic fibrosis
- Biliary stricture, gallstones, or biliary tumors
- Small bowel bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)
- Short bowel syndrome
- Radiation enteritis
- Whipple disease

In adults, the top three to screen for are usually pancreatic insufficiency, celiac disease, and bile-flow problems. Our [floating-stool guide](/poopcheck-blog/what-causes-floating-stool/) has the overlap: steatorrheic stool floats and foams for the same reason (reduced density), but adds the pale, greasy, foul-smelling features that gas alone doesn't produce.

### Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)

The pancreas secretes lipase, amylase, and protease into the small intestine. When output drops below about **10% of normal**, fat digestion fails and steatorrhea appears. StatPearls places chronic pancreatitis as the most common adult cause, with **60–90% of chronic pancreatitis patients** developing EPI within 10–12 years of diagnosis; following an episode of acute pancreatitis, EPI prevalence runs around **62% during hospitalization and 35% at follow-up** ([StatPearls on EPI](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/26572)). In children, **about 85% of cystic fibrosis patients have pancreatic insufficiency** ([StatPearls on steatorrhea](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/29474)).

The confirming test is a **fecal elastase-1** measurement; levels under 200 µg/g suggest insufficiency and under 100 µg/g confirm it. Treatment is **pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT)** taken with meals.

### Celiac disease

Gluten triggers an immune reaction that flattens the villi of the small intestine, blunting the surface area that absorbs fat and most other nutrients. NIDDK notes that digestive symptoms are more common in children than adults, that some patients have no digestive symptoms at all, and that diagnosis combines **blood tests, biopsies of the small intestine, skin biopsies, and genetic tests** ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/celiac-disease)). The treatment is a strict, lifelong gluten-free diet; NIDDK states that "following a gluten-free diet can relieve celiac disease symptoms and heal damage to the small intestine."

### SIBO and bile-flow problems

Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth deconjugates bile acids and ferments carbs higher in the gut than it should, producing both foam and some fat loss. The [SIBO explainer](/poopcheck-blog/sibo-explained/) covers the breath-test workup. Gallstones and biliary strictures reduce bile delivery, which emulsifies fat for absorption; without enough bile, fat passes through. The giveaway is clay-colored or pale stool with a greasy sheen.

### Giardiasis

A *Giardia lamblia* infection, picked up from contaminated water, daycare settings, or travel, damages the small-bowel brush border and causes greasy, foul-smelling, foamy diarrhea. Healthline notes that it usually resolves within about four weeks, with antimicrobial treatment when it doesn't ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/foamy-poop)).

## How doctors sort it out

A sensible workup escalates from history to lab to imaging:

1. **Diet and symptom history**: timing, triggers, duration, weight change, travel, antibiotics, surgery.
2. **Celiac serology** (tTG-IgA plus total IgA). The first lab to order when steatorrhea is suspected and there's no obvious pancreatic story.
3. **Fecal elastase-1** to screen for EPI.
4. **72-hour quantitative fecal fat** on a 100 g/day fat diet, the gold-standard confirmation of steatorrhea when results of the above are ambiguous.
5. **Imaging** (abdominal ultrasound, MRCP, or CT) if pancreatic or biliary pathology is suspected.
6. **Upper endoscopy with duodenal biopsy** to confirm celiac disease when serology is positive.

The pattern of symptoms and the first two labs usually narrow the picture quickly. The 7 g/day threshold on the fecal fat test is the formal line between benign froth and clinical malabsorption ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003588.htm)).

## Red flags: when to see a doctor this week

- Foamy stool lasting **more than two weeks** with no obvious food trigger
- Visible **oil, grease, or clay-colored** stool, especially if it's hard to flush
- **Unintentional weight loss**, fatigue, or new nutrient deficiency (iron, B12, vitamin D)
- **Blood or mucus** in stool, abdominal pain, or fever
- A recent travel history, antibiotics course, or new medication coinciding with the change
- A family history of celiac disease, cystic fibrosis, or pancreatic disease

Any of those moves foamy stool from a diet experiment to a workup.

## FAQ

**Is foamy stool always a sign of disease?**
No. The large majority of episodes are gas from fermentable carbs, lactose, carbonated drinks, or a brief diarrhea bout, and they resolve within days. Foam that keeps appearing for more than two weeks, especially with pale color, oiliness, or weight loss, is the one worth investigating.

**What's the difference between foamy stool and mucus in stool?**
Foam is air bubbles trapped in stool from gas production or swallowed air. Mucus is a stringy, jelly-like coating produced by the gut lining, often in response to inflammation or IBS. They can appear together, but they signal different mechanisms.

**Can IBS cause foamy stool?**
Yes. IBS, especially IBS-D, often produces frothy loose stools because fast transit and fermentable-carb sensitivity leave gas trapped in the output. The pattern usually tracks with food triggers and stress rather than with the greasy, pale, foul-smelling features of true steatorrhea.

**What foods most commonly cause foamy stool?**
High-FODMAP foods (beans, lentils, onions, garlic, apples, pears), dairy in lactose-intolerant people, sugar alcohols (sorbitol, xylitol in sugar-free gum), carbonated drinks, and a sudden jump in fiber intake. Removing the trigger clears the symptom within a few days in most cases.

**How is steatorrhea confirmed?**
With a 72-hour quantitative fecal fat collection on a controlled 100 g/day fat diet; more than 7 g of fat per 24 hours is abnormal ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003588.htm)). In practice, fecal elastase-1 for pancreatic function and celiac serology are usually ordered first because they're faster and non-invasive.

**Does foamy stool mean cancer?**
Almost never on its own. Pancreatic cancer and biliary tumors can cause steatorrhea when they obstruct enzyme or bile delivery, but they're usually accompanied by weight loss, jaundice, pain, or back discomfort, not foam alone. A thorough workup is what rules them in or out.

## The bottom line

A frothy stool after a lentil-heavy lunch is noise. Foam that keeps showing up for weeks, especially alongside grease, pallor, or weight loss, is a signal with a short differential (pancreatic insufficiency, celiac, bile flow, infection) and a workup that starts with two blood tests and a stool sample. The sooner the mechanism is named, the sooner the right fix (enzymes, a gluten-free diet, bile-salt replacement, or an antibiotic) can replace guesswork.

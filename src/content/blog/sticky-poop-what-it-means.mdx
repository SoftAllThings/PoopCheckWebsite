**Sticky poop that clings to the toilet, smears the bowl, or needs a second flush almost always traces back to one of three things: too much dietary fat, excess mucus from an irritated gut, or fat malabsorption from the pancreas or small intestine.** A single sticky day after a greasy meal is nothing. Sticky stool that keeps showing up for more than a week, especially if it's pale, foul-smelling, oily, or hard to flush, is a signal worth taking to a doctor rather than guessing at.

## Key takeaways

- **Sticky stool is a texture clue, not a diagnosis.** It can mean dietary fat, mucus, blood, or malabsorption — the pattern around it tells you which.
- **Occasional stickiness is normal.** Cleveland Clinic flags one week as the threshold where persistent sticky stool deserves a provider's attention ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop)).
- **Pale, oily, foul-smelling, hard-to-flush stool is called steatorrhea** and points to fat malabsorption. Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI), celiac disease, and chronic pancreatitis are the usual drivers ([Hospitalist/MDedge review](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/approach-exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency-considerations-diagnosis-and-treatment)).
- **Black, tarry, sticky stool is a medical urgency.** Melena is partially digested blood from the upper GI tract and needs same-day evaluation.
- **The test to ask about is faecal elastase**, not faecal fat. It's the practical first-line screen for pancreatic insufficiency ([BPAC NZ](https://bpac.org.nz/BT/2010/March/steatorrhoea.aspx)).

## What counts as "sticky"?

In day-to-day terms, sticky poop is stool that:

- Smears or streaks the toilet bowl and resists a single flush
- Clings to the paper far more than usual
- Looks oily, greasy, or shiny
- Floats and leaves an oil film on the water
- Sticks because of jelly-like mucus coating it, not grease

That bundle of symptoms is doing a lot of work. The underlying mechanisms are different, so clarifying which flavour of sticky you're seeing is the fastest route to a sensible next step. Our [stool consistency explainer](/poopcheck-blog/fiber-and-stool-consistency/) covers where "sticky" sits on the broader Bristol Scale.

## The four most common causes

### 1. Too much dietary fat

A big fried meal, a weekend of pizza and butter, or a sudden move to a high-fat ketogenic eating pattern can produce greasy, pale, buoyant stool for a day or two. Your pancreas is fine — it's just that the fat load outran your bile and enzyme output. Cleveland Clinic lists fried foods, red meat, and whole-fat dairy as the three most common dietary triggers ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop)).

The giveaway: it resolves within 24–72 hours of returning to a normal diet. If it doesn't, something else is going on.

### 2. Excess mucus from an irritated gut

Your intestines produce mucus every day as a lubricant and protective layer. In the right amount, you never see it. When the gut is inflamed or irritated, mucus output rises and shows up visibly in stool. Common causes include IBS, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's and ulcerative colitis), food intolerances, hemorrhoids, and GI infections such as *C. difficile* ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop)).

Mucus-driven stickiness tends to look stringy, jelly-like, and translucent rather than greasy. We cover the full spectrum of healthy vs. worrying patterns in our [mucus in stool guide](/poopcheck-blog/mucus-in-stool/).

### 3. Fat malabsorption (steatorrhea)

When fat makes it to the colon undigested, you get **steatorrhea** — pale, bulky, oily, foul-smelling stool that floats, sticks, and takes two flushes. The clinical definition is a fat-absorption coefficient under 93% measured over a 72-hour collection ([Hospitalist/MDedge review](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/approach-exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency-considerations-diagnosis-and-treatment)). You won't measure that at home, but the look and smell are consistent enough that gastroenterologists can usually pattern-match it in a visit.

The common causes:

- **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)** — the pancreas doesn't release enough lipase, protease, and amylase. Chronic pancreatitis is the most frequent cause in adults; cystic fibrosis is the main driver in children ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop)).
- **Celiac disease** — gluten triggers immune damage to the small-intestine villi, blunting fat absorption even in people with "silent" presentations.
- **Bile acid issues** — gallbladder removal, Crohn's disease of the terminal ileum, or cholestatic liver disease all cut the bile delivery that fat needs to be emulsified.
- **Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)** — bacteria deconjugate bile acids before they can do their job. See our [SIBO explainer](/poopcheck-blog/sibo-explained/) for the longer picture.

### 4. Blood from upper GI bleeding (melena)

Sticky, black, and tarry is a different diagnosis entirely. **Melena** is partially digested blood from the stomach, duodenum, or upper small intestine. The classic cause is a bleeding peptic ulcer. Dark iron supplements and bismuth (Pepto-Bismol) can mimic the colour but not the tar-like texture. Our [black stool causes](/poopcheck-blog/black-stool-causes/) explainer covers how to tell them apart.

Melena is urgent. Not next-week urgent — same-day urgent.

## The clinical signs that point to malabsorption

Gastroenterologists use a short pattern to separate "ate too much fat" from "pancreas isn't keeping up":

- **Oil droplets** that visibly separate from the stool mass and become whitish and firm as they cool. BPAC NZ calls this a strong clinical pointer toward pancreatic insufficiency on history alone ([BPAC NZ](https://bpac.org.nz/BT/2010/March/steatorrhoea.aspx)).
- **Pale or clay-coloured** stool across multiple days.
- **Foul, rancid smell** stronger than normal stool.
- **Unintentional weight loss** of a few kg or more over weeks to months.
- **Signs of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency** — easy bruising (K), night-vision changes (A), bone pain (D) — in longer-standing cases ([PubMed review, Fat digestion and absorption](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38429963/)).

## Red flags: when to see a doctor

Book a visit this week, not next month, if sticky stool shows up alongside any of these:

- **Persistent sticky stool for more than a week** without an obvious diet change ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop))
- **Black, tarry, sticky stool** — same-day evaluation, not next week
- **Unintentional weight loss** or steady, unexplained fatigue
- **Visible blood** in or on the stool, bright red or dark
- **Chronic diarrhea or loose stools** for more than a week ([Medical News Today / NHS summary](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/320700))
- **Pale, oily, buoyant stool** that keeps showing up — the classic malabsorption pattern
- **New-onset symptoms after age 45**, especially with any [pencil-thin stools](/poopcheck-blog/pencil-thin-stools-when-to-worry/) or changes in frequency

Ask for a **faecal elastase** test if EPI is on the table. BPAC NZ and other guidelines favour it over the older faecal fat quantification, which has poor sensitivity for pancreatic insufficiency ([BPAC NZ](https://bpac.org.nz/BT/2010/March/steatorrhoea.aspx)). Celiac serology (tissue transglutaminase IgA) is the sensible parallel test if there's any suspicion of coeliac disease.

## What you can try first

With no red flags, run a clean two-week experiment before booking a visit. Cut fried foods, full-fat dairy, and fatty cuts of meat for 10–14 days. Hold fibre at 25–30 g a day from mixed sources (our [fibre and stool consistency](/poopcheck-blog/fiber-and-stool-consistency/) guide walks through the ramp). Hydrate at 2–3 L of fluid a day. Log every bowel movement so you have a before-and-after pattern, not a vibe. Then reintroduce fat slowly at the end of the fortnight.

If stool normalises when you drop the fat and returns when you reintroduce it, that's a dietary trigger, not a disease. If nothing changes, that's your cue to ask for the pancreatic and coeliac workup rather than keep guessing.

## FAQ

**Is sticky poop always a sign of malabsorption?**
No. The most common cause by far is a short-term high-fat diet. Malabsorption usually comes with pale colour, foul smell, weight loss, and persistence beyond a week ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sticky-poop)).

**What's the difference between sticky poop and mucus in stool?**
Sticky-greasy stool is usually about fat (either dietary or malabsorbed). Mucus looks stringy, jelly-like, and translucent, and typically points to gut irritation or IBS/IBD rather than fat. Our [mucus in stool guide](/poopcheck-blog/mucus-in-stool/) covers the mucus side in depth.

**Does sticky poop mean I have EPI?**
Not on its own. EPI usually presents as pale, oily, foul-smelling, buoyant stool that persists, plus weight loss or fat-soluble vitamin deficiency. The screening test is faecal elastase, not a stool-fat quantification ([BPAC NZ](https://bpac.org.nz/BT/2010/March/steatorrhoea.aspx)).

**Why does my poop stick to the toilet bowl?**
Usually dietary fat or excess mucus. If it also floats, is pale, and is hard to flush, that's the malabsorption pattern — worth a GI visit.

**Can keto cause sticky stool?**
Yes, briefly. A sudden jump in dietary fat can outrun the pancreas and bile for a few days, especially before the gut adapts. If it persists past two or three weeks on a stable keto diet, treat it like any other persistent sticky stool.

**Is sticky stool a sign of colon cancer?**
Stickiness alone isn't a classic colon-cancer sign. The more specific red flags are rectal bleeding, [pencil-thin stools](/poopcheck-blog/pencil-thin-stools-when-to-worry/), persistent change in bowel habits, and unintentional weight loss. Any of those warrant evaluation.

## The bottom line

Sticky poop is a texture, not a diagnosis. One greasy day after a greasy meal is noise. A week or more of pale, oily, buoyant, hard-to-flush stool is a signal — specifically, a signal to screen for fat malabsorption with a faecal elastase and, where appropriate, celiac serology. Black and tarry sticks together but belongs in a separate box: same-day urgency, upper-GI bleed until proven otherwise. For everyone else, the two-week fat-trial is a reasonable first move before the lab visit.
